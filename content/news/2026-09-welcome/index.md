This autumn, the FROST team came together at Ghent University's Department of Archaeology. With two PhD researchers now on board, the project's first fieldwork and laboratory work is getting under way.

FROST asks how hunter-gatherers in Western Europe coped with the **Younger Dryas**, an abrupt return to near-glacial conditions around 12,850 years ago that lasted for more than a millennium. Over the coming years, the team will combine climate records from cave formations, sediment cores from peat bogs and former lakes, isotope analysis of reindeer teeth and new dating methods at around 30 sites in five countries.

**Stefan Bryant** will reconstruct Younger Dryas landscapes from sediments, pollen and radiocarbon-dated plant remains ([work package 2](../../research/wp2-palaeoenvironment/)). **Arne Willocx** will work on the timing of human occupation, combining OSL dating with traces of invisible volcanic ash ([work package 4](../../research/wp4-chronology/)).

Follow this page for fieldwork diaries, new results and events.
