**Aim:** to integrate the results of all FROST work packages and to test how climate and environmental change during the Younger Dryas affected human populations in Western Europe.

## Approach

- **Palaeodemographic reconstruction.** Using a "dates as data" approach, the combined set of new and critically evaluated radiocarbon dates will be used to reconstruct changes in the intensity of human activity through time, separately for the western and eastern parts of the study area. This provides a direct test of the hypothesis of an early Younger Dryas population decline followed by recovery.
- **Chronological integration.** The palaeoclimate, palaeoenvironmental, reindeer and archaeological records will be placed on a common chronological framework, enabling direct comparison of changes in temperature, hydroclimate and vegetation with changes in human activity. GIS will be used to explore spatial patterns across the landscape.
- **Eco-cultural niche modelling.** Statistical models will combine environmental variables with cultural parameters, such as mobility and subsistence strategies, to map habitable areas, probable movement corridors and potential refugia during each phase of the Younger Dryas.
- **Supra-regional comparison.** Results from Western Europe will be compared with those from Scandinavia, Central Europe and the Mediterranean, to assess whether the observed patterns are regional or part of a continent-wide process.

## Outputs

An integrated assessment of the impact of the Younger Dryas on human populations in Western Europe, comprising palaeodemographic models and predictive maps of recolonisation, together with a test of whether population responses varied along a north-west to south-east gradient.
