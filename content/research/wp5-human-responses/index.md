**Aim:** to combine all of FROST's results and test how climate and environmental change during the Younger Dryas affected human populations in Western Europe.

## How we are tackling it

- **Population change.** Using the "dates as data" approach, the full set of new and reliable published radiocarbon dates is used to track rises and falls in human activity over time, separately for the western and eastern parts of the study area. This directly tests the idea of an early Younger Dryas decline followed by recovery.
- **One shared timeline.** The climate, environmental, reindeer and archaeological records are placed on a single timeline, so that changes in temperature, rainfall and vegetation can be compared directly with changes in human activity, using GIS to explore patterns across the landscape.
- **Eco-cultural niche modelling.** Statistical models combine environmental conditions with cultural factors such as mobility and subsistence to map where people could live, likely migration corridors and possible refuges in each phase of the Younger Dryas.
- **The wider picture.** Results from Western Europe will be compared with Scandinavia, Central Europe and the Mediterranean to see whether the patterns are regional or part of a Europe-wide story.

## What it will deliver

A comprehensive picture of how the Younger Dryas affected people in Western Europe, including palaeodemographic models and predictive maps of recolonisation, and a test of whether responses varied from north-west to south-east.

## Who

The whole FROST team.
